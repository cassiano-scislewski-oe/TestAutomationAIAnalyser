3. Failure Patterns and Anti-patterns

Recurring Patterns:

  • Cross-Locale Functional Regression: The most prominent pattern is the failure of the *same test case ID* (e.g., C1974026, C1685062, C1958791) across multiple geographical locales (US, AU, CA, IT, NL). This strongly suggests a regression in a core, shared application feature or a fundamental localization component, rather than isolated automation errors specific to one locale.
  • Module-Wide Instability (DropShip, Order History, Register Bike): Specific modules, such as DropShip, Transaction History, and Service/Register Bike, exhibit multiple failures. This indicates areas of the application that may have recently undergone significant changes, lack sufficient test coverage, or suffer from underlying architectural instability.
  • Early & Hard Stops (BROKEN status): Tests marked as 'BROKEN', particularly within the DropShip module, often suggest a critical setup failure or an environmental issue that prevents the test from proceeding past initial steps. These are typically more severe than 'FAILED' tests, which might encounter an assertion failure later in the flow.

Anti-patterns:

  • Lack of Granular Error Isolation: While the test names indicate specific features, the widespread nature of failures for the same test IDs across locales hints at a potential lack of granular error messages or diagnostic data that clearly differentiates application bugs from environmental or test data issues.
  • Insufficient Pre-Test Health Checks: The presence of multiple 'BROKEN' tests and recurring functional regressions across locales suggests that the test suite might not be adequately validating critical external dependencies, backend service availability, or core environment configurations *before* commencing the full execution. This leads to wasted test execution time on already compromised environments.
  • Brittle Test Data/Environment Configuration: Many failures related to "invalid serial number", "export lines from date range", or "completed orders" imply that the test data or environment configurations might be fragile and susceptible to changes, leading to unexpected test outcomes.
